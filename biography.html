01 . PERSONAL STORY

BIOGRAPHY OF MARIE CURIE

Marie Curie, born Maria Salomea Skłodowska on 7 November 1867 in Warsaw, Poland, was one of the most influential scientists in history. She was a brilliant physicist and chemist whose remarkable discoveries changed the world of science forever. From an early age, Marie showed a deep interest in education, mathematics, and scientific research. She was born into a family of teachers who encouraged learning and knowledge. However, growing up in Poland during a time of political difficulties and limited educational opportunities for women was not easy. Despite these challenges, she remained determined to achieve her dreams and never allowed difficulties to stop her from pursuing education. In 1891, Marie moved to Paris, France, to continue her higher education at the University of Paris, also known as the Sorbonne. She studied physics and mathematics and worked extremely hard to achieve academic success. Her life in Paris was financially difficult, but her passion for science kept her focused on her goals. During this period, she met Pierre Curie, a talented physicist who shared her interest in scientific research. They married in 1895 and became one of the most remarkable scientific partnerships in history. Together, they began conducting experiments that would eventually lead to discoveries that transformed modern science. Marie Curie became particularly interested in the study of radiation and began investigating the properties of uranium. Through her careful experiments, she discovered that radiation originated from within atoms. She introduced the term radioactivity to describe this phenomenon. Her research led to one of the greatest scientific breakthroughs of the nineteenth century. In 1898, Marie and Pierre Curie discovered two new chemical elements, Polonium and Radium. Marie named Polonium after her homeland, Poland, while Radium was named because of its radioactive properties. These discoveries greatly expanded scientific understanding and opened new possibilities for research in physics and chemistry. Marie Curie's extraordinary achievements soon brought her international recognition. In 1903, she received the Nobel Prize in Physics, which she shared with Pierre Curie and Henri Becquerel for their research on radiation. She became the first woman to receive a Nobel Prize, making history in the scientific world. Sadly, Pierre Curie died in 1906, but Marie continued their research with great determination. She later became the first woman to serve as a professor at the University of Paris. In 1911, she received her second Nobel Prize, this time in Chemistry, for her discoveries and research on Radium and Polonium. She became the first person in history to win Nobel Prize.
